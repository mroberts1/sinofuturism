Han's argument in five chapters, each organized around a Chinese character: Chinese thought is deconstructionist *from the outset* — not a Western philosophical move applied to an alien tradition, but the native shape of Far Eastern thinking. The book uses the contemporary shanzhai (counterfeit/fake) phenomenon as its entry point, then traces the deep cultural logic behind it.

This is the philosophical foundation beneath Lawrence Lek's manifesto claim that "copy everything, authorship is overrated, copyright is wrong" — Han shows why this is not nihilism but the expression of a different ontology.

## Chapters

### 权 Quan — Law

*Quan* literally means the sliding weight on a scale: moveable, adjustable, provisional. As the root of the Chinese words for rights, intellectual property, and copyright, it inscribes relativity and situativity into those concepts from the start. Han contrasts this with the Western metaphysics of Being — Platonic truth as immutable, Hegelian substance, the god who "stands for truth and truthfulness." Chinese thought substitutes the *sliding weight of quan* for the *gravitation of Being*; situation replaces substance.

Hegel's accusation that the Chinese have an innate tendency to lie is his misreading of anti-essentialism. What looks like moral abandonment from within a metaphysics of fixed identity is simply the pragmatism of a thought that trusts no invariable essences.

### 真跡 Zhen Ji — Original

*Zhen ji* means "authentic trace" — and the structure of that phrase undoes the Western concept of the original. A trace does not condense into a monomorphic presence; it differs from itself, keeps the artwork from achieving a final form. Chinese masterpieces are palimpsests: connoisseurs and collectors inscribe themselves into the work via seals and commentaries. *The origin turns out to be a retrospective construction.*

Case study: Chang Dai-chien, the twentieth century's most celebrated Chinese painter, whose career was built on forgeries of Old Masters that were arguably more authentic than the originals. His "forgeries" of lost works known only from written descriptions created originals that had never existed. Han's conclusion: *the greater a master, the emptier his oeuvre*.

Contrast with Adorno on Wagner: Adorno sees the artwork as living and changing, grounded in *inner depth and fullness*. The Chinese artwork changes because it is in itself *empty and flat* — the outwardness of tradition and situation drives change, not inward essence.

### 閑章 Xian Zhan — Seals of Leisure

Chinese seal stamps are not signatures in the Western sense. They do not finalize authorship or prohibit intervention; they *open a communicative field*. Art is sociable and communal — a farewell painting accumulates poems and seals from all present, each inscribing themselves into the work.

Han opposes two image-types: the Chinese **image of absence** (aperspective, asubjective, without soul or gaze) against the Western **image of presence** (Van Eyck's *Arnolfini Portrait* as paradigm — signature, date, convex mirror, the divine gaze, consolidating authorial presence at the center). European signatures are *seals of finito*; Chinese stamps are invitations.

### 複製 Fuzhi — Copy

Two Chinese copy-concepts: *fangzhipin* (obvious imitation, museum-shop model) and *fuzhipin* (exact reproduction of equal value, no negative connotation). Western museums regularly misread the latter as deception.

The Ise Shrine: the supreme Shinto sanctuary, considered 1,300 years old, is completely rebuilt every twenty years. UNESCO removed it from the World Heritage list — for UNESCO it is at most twenty years old. Han's inversion: *the copy is more original than the original*, since the older a building gets, the further it is from its original state. Reproduction is a technique of preservation; it is how identity persists *through* death rather than *against* it — the Buddhist logic of cycles over *creatio ex nihilo*.

The terra-cotta warriors: manufactured through modular mass production from the start. Modular production opposes uniqueness to maximize reproducibility; it *modulates* the same to create difference, working like nature rather than like genius.

### 山寨 Shanzhai — Fake

[[shanzhai]] (literally "mountain stronghold," after the outlaws of *Water Margin*) as the contemporary form of Chinese creative logic: not crude forgery but playful, transformative variation. Shanzhai phones add functions the originals lack. Labels mutate — Adidas becomes Adidos, Adadas, Adadis — in what Han calls a "Dadaist game" that parodies economic monopoly.

**Shanzhai is [[decreation]]**: it opposes *creatio ex nihilo* with process; Being with path; identity with transformational difference. It manifests the genuinely Chinese spirit. Maoism as shanzhai Marxism — transforming Marx's doctrine in the absence of an industrial proletariat. Chinese "both-and" versus Western "either-or": contradiction is not a Chinese concept.

Han closes with the possibility of *shanzhai democracy* — not as a utopian hope but as the logical extrapolation of a political system already showing hybrid characteristics.

## Key Concepts

- **[[decreation]]** (*Ent-schöpfung*): The desubstantializing operation that empties out Being. Not destruction but the removal of essence; what remains is process and trace.
- **[[the-trace]]** (*ji*, 跡): The unit of the Chinese original. Neither presence nor sign; it keeps the work open to continual rearrangement.
- **[[process-vs-being]]**: Western metaphysics founded on Being (immutable, self-identical); Chinese thought founded on process (the Tao, continuous change).
- Emptiness (*kong*): Not nihilistic nothingness but the negativity of decreation — it desubstantializes Being and opens the work to inscription.
- Image of absence vs image of presence: Chinese images are aperspective, asubjective; Western images consolidate authorial subjectivity at the center.

## Relation to Sinofuturism

Han provides the philosophical archaeology that Lek's manifesto and the *Machine Decision* essays draw on without always citing. [[shanzhai]] in the KB treats the contemporary tech phenomenon; this book explains why that phenomenon has the cultural logic it does. The connections are direct:

- [[rengong-zhineng]]: Han's analysis of *fuzhipin* (reproduction of equal value) reframes the "humanmade" intelligence concept — copying is not degraded intelligence but a different relation to originality
- [[cosmotechnics]]: Han and [[yuk-hui]] converge — both argue Chinese thought operates on a different ontological register from Western metaphysics, with direct implications for technology
- [[lek-sinofuturism-1839-2046]]: "Copy everything. Authorship is overrated. Copyright is wrong" is precisely Han's *quan* — the sliding weight of IP that cannot fix essence
