Afterword to *Machine Decision Is Not Final* (Urbanomic, 2025), by co-editor Benjamin Bratton. The title carries both senses of "apology": a defense of a position, and an expression of regret. Bratton offers Chinese readers an explanation of why Western AI discourse responded to DeepSeek with surprise and discombobulation — by exposing the provincial assumptions baked into Western AI theory.

## Starting Point: DeepSeek

The DeepSeek moment (early 2025) is the occasion. DeepSeek was "Street AI: fast, cheap, good, and easy to use" — which made Western AI "look downright bloated and locked down in comparison." More importantly, it suggested that AI development could follow evolutionary paths irreducible to its instrumentality for any state's or corporation's ends. "Just as life finds a way to evolve, so does technology — because technology is a kind of life."

Bratton frames this as confirmation that the AI landscape is not adequately described by Western categories — and that Western AI theory has failed to understand even itself, let alone China.

## What is Chinese Theory of AI?

Bratton identifies three concerns central to Chinese AI theory (while insisting these are open-ended, not essentialist):

1. **Location of AI among wu (物, things)** — the constellation of Confucian and Daoist ethical relations involving all things; what obligations does AI generate, and to whom?
2. **Intelligence as coordinated and automatised production** — not the simulation of an individual mind but the coordination of minds and bodies in collective worldmaking (connecting to [[rengong-zhineng]])
3. **AI and governance** — the Chinese state is arguably the primary AI; the concept of the "state" does not map symmetrically onto Western counterparts; see also Garton's Han Feizi essay

Bratton's account of [[qian-xuesen]] is particularly strong: the figure who co-founded Caltech's JPL, was deported as a security threat, and then brought cybernetics to China — embodying the violent disentanglement of US-China techno-scientific co-constitution.

The "Four New Great Inventions" (xin si da faming) — high-speed rail, e-commerce, mobile payments, bike-sharing — are all implementations, not inventions. This raises the reverse Needham question: might the West now be stuck in a high-equilibrium trap, its decentralized markets and individualist intelligence culture making societal-scale AI implementation harder than for China?

## Seven Motifs of Western AI Mystification

Bratton catalogs recurring flaws in Western AI theory:

1. **Natural vs. artificial** — Western obsession with authenticity frames AI as counterfeit; in Chinese, rengong (humanmade) carries no such negative charge
2. **Individual consciousness as the measure** — intelligence is located in the qualia of individual experience; AI can only "pass" if it simulates one subject
3. **The embodiment riposte** — AI is "disembodied"; but the embodiment invoked is phenomenological, not biological — it reinstates the dualism it claims to overcome
4. **Sociomorphism** — from "AI mirrors individual intelligence" to "AI mirrors social power structures"; AI is what Power does; any suggestion it might not be sociomorphic must be suppressed
5. **Freedom vs. control** — AI is positioned on an eternal liberal spectrum; command and control trigger automatic resistance narratives
6. **Fiction/reality blurring** — fictional portrayals of AI are analyzed as if they were AI; textual interpretation passes as political challenge
7. **Eschatology** — AI is either salvation or apocalypse; the American tradition of social panic (stop buttons, data center bombings, Roko's Basilisk) is the eschatological form

## The Planetary Theory of AI

The conclusion is not Western-centric critique transplanted to China but the collective construction of **a planetary theory of AI** — acknowledging that existing models of "artificial intelligence" are multiple without collapsing into cultural essentialism or false universalism.

"China and AI cannot be reduced to a single, uniform, or consistent narrative." Better translations are needed "between different ways of defining what AI is and what it is for." The final move: both human and machine cultural forms must be situated within "the twisting evolution of intelligence as a fully planetary process that is now staring back at us."
