Foreword by [[yuk-hui]] to a special issue of *Angelaki: Journal of the Theoretical Humanities* (vol. 25, no. 4, August 2020) dedicated to cosmotechnics. Two pages; the most concise and authoritative statement of the concept available. The special issue was funded by the Cosmotechnics/Critical AI project at City University of Hong Kong.

## The Precise Definition

> I gave a preliminary definition of cosmotechnics as **unification between the cosmic order and the moral order through technical activities**, in order to suggest that technology should be re-situated in a broader reality, which enables it and also constrains it.

This is the canonical formulation. Technology is not free-standing but embedded in a cosmic-moral reality that both enables and constrains it. The detachment of technology from this reality — its claim to universality and self-grounding — is what Hui diagnoses as the problem of modern Western technology.

## Three Disciplinary Challenges

Hui frames cosmotechnics as addressing the limits of three twentieth-century traditions:

**Philosophy of technology (Heidegger):** The 1949 Bremen lecture distinguishes Greek *technē* (essence in *poiesis*, bringing-forth) from modern technology (enframing, *Gestell* — world as standing reserve). But where does ancient Indian, Chinese, or Amazonian technology fit? These cannot be assimilated to Greek *technē* nor equated with modern technology — Heidegger's binary leaves them unthinkable.

**Anthropology of technology (Leroi-Gourhan):** Technics as extension of organs and externalization of memory makes technology anthropologically universal — proceeding from what Leroi-Gourhan called a "technical tendency." This is not wrong, but it fails to explain "technical facts," which differ from region to region and culture to culture. What is embedded in these differences beyond causal reduction to cultural variation or contingency?

**History of technology (Needham):** The famous question — why wasn't modern science and technology developed in China and India, despite China's extensive scientific/technological development before the sixteenth century? The distorted version of this question treats it as a comparison of advancement levels. The correct reading: Chinese and Western science and technology are based on *different epistemologies and philosophies* and cannot be directly compared. The question is how to re-articulate these differences.

## Mono-Technological Culture and the Anthropocene

Colonization, modernization, and globalization — accompanied by economic growth and military expansion — produced a **mono-technological culture** in which modern Western technology becomes the principle productive force, determining relations between human and non-human beings, human and cosmos, nature and culture. The consequences: exhaustion of resources, destruction of the environment — the discourse of the Anthropocene.

This is the stakes of the argument: the quest for multiple cosmotechnics is not merely a philosophical pluralism but a response to ecological catastrophe driven by a single universalizing technical paradigm.

## Multiple Cosmotechnics

The goal is not one alternative cosmotechnics but **multiple cosmotechnics** — a plurality of ways of situating technical activity within different cosmic-moral orders. The special issue brings together contributions exploring this plurality across different traditions and regions.
