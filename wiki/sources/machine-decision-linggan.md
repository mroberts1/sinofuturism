Second section of *Machine Decision Is Not Final* (Urbanomic, 2025). Organized around 灵感 (linggan) — inspiration, creative epiphany, the non-discursive cognitive leap. Three essays focus on [[qian-xuesen]] and the strange history of Chinese cybernetics; a fourth reads Han Feizi legalism as a Daoist theory of AI governance.

## Bo An — "Between the Human and the Machinic: Qian Xuesen and AI"

Detailed account of Qian Xuesen's attempt to engineer [[linggan]] — to close the gap between algorithmic computation and the mysterious leaps of human creativity. Qian's 思维科学 (siwei kexue, "noetic science") proposed that inspiration could be studied mechanistically and eventually programmed.

Bo An shows how Qian's translation of "virtual reality" as 灵境 (lingjing, "spirit realm") emerged from this long-standing interest in the ephemeral and incorporeal. The religious charge in the character ling — spaces of divine immanence, mountain caves, shrines — suggests that for Qian, virtual reality was not a simulation of physical space but a portal through which imponderable forces could operate.

## You Mi — "A Different Endpoint: Qian Xuesen and Cosmotechnics"

You Mi focuses on Qian's 1980s work during the "qigong fever" period, when Reform and Opening-up (改革开放) coincided with mass somatic-spiritual practice. Where Western cyberneticists were theorizing disembodied information processing, Qian was connecting his computational vision to the body's "special capacities."

The essay is organized around a hyperstitional film by artist Shi Qing, commissioned for the 2020 Shanghai Biennale, which reconstrues Qian's unrealized possibilities as a live alternative trajectory. You Mi imagines qigong and renti kexue (人体科学, somatic science) "written back" into computation — a [[cosmotechnics]] in which nature and artifice, human and machine, are harmonized rather than opposed.

## Wang Hongzhe — "Five Moments in the History of Chinese Cybernetics"

The most historically comprehensive essay in the section. See [[chinese-cybernetics]] for the full account. Five episodes: Wiener's 1935 Beijing visit; Qian's ocean crossing; the Cultural Revolution suppression; Deng-era revival intertwined with qigong and paranormal research; the poet Hai Zi's death and its Daoist witchcraft accusations.

Wang's thesis: these "forgotten footnotes" to Chinese cybernetics history hold the key to discovering alternative cybernetic futures — paths not taken that remain latent and potentially reactivatable.

## Vincent Garton — "Automaticity and the Mystery of State"

The section's political-philosophical essay. Garton reads the Han Feizi — a third-century BC text of Chinese political thought — alongside Hobbes's Leviathan to produce a theory of AI governance grounded in Daoist cosmology.

Hobbes's Leviathan is "an Artificiall Man" and sovereignty an "Artificiall Soul" — making the modern state, by definition, an AI that dominates its creators. But Hobbes's sovereign retains a theological excess even in a supposedly secular modernity. The Han Feizi, Garton argues, offers a different cosmology: sovereign power grounded not in transcendent authority but in identification with the **mysterious emptiness of the Dao** — immanent, automatic, non-willful.

This reframes linggan (inspiration) as a political concept: governance that operates through wu wei (effortless action) rather than command, automatic and invisible rather than visible and directive. Bratton's suggestion that "the state in China is the primary AI" is given philosophical depth here. "A new mode of political thought remains to be invented," Garton concludes.
