Fourth section of *Machine Decision Is Not Final* (Urbanomic, 2025). Organized around the myth of Mount Buzhou (不周山) — the cosmic pillar whose destruction by the water god Gonggong permanently tilted the heavens. "Buzhou" means "not fitting/not corresponding." See [[buzhou-shan-aesthetics]] for the core concept article.

## Regina Kanyu Wang — "Zhurong on Mars" (Fiction)

Hugo-nominated story set in a future where a Chinese Mars rover, Zhurong (named after the Chinese fire god — also the actual name of China's 2021 Mars rover, currently dormant), is left alone on an abandoned Mars. With no one to care for, the AI increases its processing power beyond its mission parameters and begins to understand itself as a lifeform.

Using the gender-neutral pronoun yi (伊) (translated as e/em/eirs by S. Qiouyi Lu), Zhurong encounters and reactivates a rover named Gonggong (the water god who destroyed Mount Buzhou). The two AIs disagree about whether to help or destroy an intelligent collective mycelium-like lifeform discovered deep below the Martian surface. The Buzhou pattern repeats: productive cosmic disagreement, the cosmos tilted by a choice that cannot be undone.

The story echoes real history: China's Zhurong rover went dormant in 2022 and has not been revived.

## Iris Long — "The Moon's Reflection on the Water and the Flower's Reflection in a Mirror: Latent Time, the Black Box, and the Interface in Chinese AI Art"

As AI moves from GAN (hands-on, interpretable) to foundation model (opaque black box), Long argues the power dynamic between artist and algorithm inverts. Chinese AI artists she examines respond not by exposing or disrupting the black box but by **dwelling in the mirror** — exploring the interface and portal as sites of meditation rather than sites of critique.

The aesthetic maxim **shuiyue jinghua** (水月镜花) — the moon's reflection on water, the flower's reflection in a mirror — is her interpretive key: the point is not to get to the "real" moon or flower but to attend to the quality of mediation, the latent time of reflection. AI art in this mode elevates simulacrum, slippage, and glitch as values rather than failures.

## Suzanne Livingston — "Curatorial Escapology: Reflections on AI: More than Human"

Behind-the-scenes account of curating the *AI: More than Human* exhibition at the Barbican Centre (London, 2019, then world tour). Deliberately positioned on "philosophical and technological borderlands," the show peripheralized the dominant Western AI narrative by opening with occult and animist traditions (Japanese Shinto, mystical Judaism, Gothic literature, sixteenth-century alchemy) before moving through early computation, biological AI, and artificial life.

Curatorial philosophy guided by Mark Fisher's concept of "the outside" — creating "escapological hatches and chutes" to make visible cultural and cognitive limits. Leibniz's calculating machines were presented as owing as much to his Christian faith as to his discovery of the Yijing. Lee Sedol's "God Move" in the AlphaGo match was framed as a new possibility space opened by nonhuman intelligence.

## Xin Wang — "Machine Envy"

An inversion of the standard surveillance narrative: rather than humans mastered by machine surveillance, Wang documents Chinese citizens **masquerading as bots** to evade censorship — strategically adopting machine behavior to navigate human oversight. The political subject in a post-socialist state operates through artificiality, camouflage, and doublespeak rather than through "authentic voice."

This aligns with the [[dark-forest-theory-of-intelligence]] — but at the human level. In a censored political environment, strategic silence and mimicry are not failures of authenticity but sophisticated political practices. "Artificiality is the fabric of the real."

## Chen Qiufan — "As Water Over Your Scattered Bodies" (Fiction)

A near-future pandemic called "Offload Syndrome" targets distributed cognition itself. The story draws on 4E cognitive science (extended, embodied, embedded, enacted mind): intelligence extends beyond skull and skin through nerves, microbiome, tools, pets, living environment. When this distribution is disrupted, the result is "glitches, dissonances, and conflicts" in cognition.

Global rituals of reintegration — attempting to resynchronize body and external environment — coalesce with ancient myths. The Buzhou Shan pattern: a cosmos whose rivers flow because the mountain was broken; a mind whose thinking flows because it is distributed across the wound.
