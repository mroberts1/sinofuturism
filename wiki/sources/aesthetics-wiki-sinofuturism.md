A community-curated entry on the Aesthetics Wiki (`aesthetics.fandom.com`) treating Sinofuturism as a *visual aesthetic* rather than strictly an art-theoretical movement. Useful as a complement to the Lek-Greenspan-Konior theoretical framing: it captures how Sinofuturism circulates in the wider online aesthetics-taxonomy ecosystem — alongside Cottagecore, Cybergoth, Solarpunk, etc. — and what visual signifiers and media artifacts community curators have crystallized around the term.

The wiki entry treats "Sinofuturism" and "Chinese Sci-Fi Futurism" (中国科幻未来主义) as interchangeable, distinguishing them only by emphasis: Sinofuturism as a Western-theorized, often ironic-or-critical art movement; Chinese Sci-Fi Futurism as a domestic literary descriptor "characterised by faith in kindness, optimism, collectivism, profound homeland and national sentiments."

## Core claims

- **Lek's seven stereotypes are reframed as the founding schema.** The wiki summarizes Lek's 2016 essay film around seven stereotypes Western observers hold about contemporary China: Calculation, Replication, Gaming, Learning, Addiction, Labor, Gambling. (Compare Lek's original phrasing in [[lek-sinofuturism-1839-2046]] — Computing/Copying/Gaming/Studying/Addiction/Labor/Gambling. The wiki paraphrases.)

- **Afrofuturism is the explicit parent.** "This futurist movement is also inspired by Afrofuturism." This confirms the lineage Lek himself draws in the founding essay.

- **The Three-Body Problem moment matters.** The wiki treats Liu Cixin's emergence in global SF as the threshold event after which Sinofuturism "took on richer interpretations." For a wiki entry on the aesthetic, the canonical literary anchor is *not* Lek's film but Liu Cixin's trilogy.

- **An accelerationist reading is named explicitly.** "Artists view the explosive growth of contemporary China following the reform and opening-up from an accelerationist perspective." This is the same lineage [[concepts/sinofuturism]] gestures at via Nick Land and the CCRU — here treated as a settled feature of the movement, not a contested ancestry.

- **The visual shift: from rockets to surveillance.** The aesthetic's "design imagination" has moved away from Soviet-coded space-and-rockets imagery toward "more realistic and actionable domains such as smart cities and digital surveillance." Two registers coexist: the "ghost towns" of hollowed-out northern industrial cities, and the "intelligent metropolises" of southern first-tier cities.

- **"Surreal streetscape" as the audio-visual signature.** The wiki names a specific look: "the surreal streetscape aesthetic seen in sci-fi films" emerging from China's actual urban and rural landscapes. This is the cinematographic register the entry treats as Sinofuturism's primary visual fingerprint.

## Media canon (per the wiki)

Films, novels, and artworks the entry lists as exemplary:

- **Liu Cixin** — *The Three-Body Problem* trilogy
- **The Wandering Earth** film series (2019, 2023)
- **Shi Jiepeng (史杰鹏) & Xu Ruotao (徐若涛)** — *玉门* (Yumen)
- **Qiu Anxiong (邱黯雄)** — *新山海经* (The New Book of Mountains and Seas) and *新山海经三3*
- **Cao Fei (曹斐)** — *红霞* (Red Glow); *谁的乌托邦* (Whose Utopia, 2006)
- **Fan Wennan (范文南)** — *中国2098* (China 2098)
- **Arknights: Endfield** (video game, Hypergryph)

This is a substantially different canon from the Anglophone academic sources (Lek, Hui, Han, Machine Decision) — heavily weighted toward Chinese-language film, video art, and SF, with figures (Qiu Anxiong, Cao Fei, Fan Wennan) who are major in the Chinese contemporary art world but largely absent from the Anglophone theoretical literature on Sinofuturism.

## Relevance to the wiki

This entry sits at a different elevation than the rest of the source pool. It's *not* peer-reviewed scholarship or artist manifesto; it's vernacular, community-curated, ESL-edited, citation-light. But it does three useful things the other sources do not:

1. **Surfaces a Chinese-language artistic canon** (Cao Fei, Qiu Anxiong, Fan Wennan, Shi Jiepeng) that should eventually become source entries in their own right.
2. **Locates Sinofuturism inside the broader online aesthetics-taxonomy ecosystem** (Guochao, Silkpunk, Y3K, Neo-Chinese Style as siblings) — a horizontal mapping the academic sources don't provide.
3. **Documents the contemporary visual register**: red/blue/beige/metallics, surveillance over rockets, ghost towns vs. intelligent metropolises. Concrete colour and motif vocabulary that's useful for visual analysis.

## Cross-aesthetic neighbours

The "Related aesthetics" the entry lists are worth investigating as potential future wiki entries — each is its own Aesthetics Wiki article:

- **Afrofuturism** — already named in [[concepts/sinofuturism]] as Sinofuturism's parent lineage
- **Guochao (国潮)** — domestic Chinese cultural-confidence aesthetic; commercial-design adjacent
- **Indigenous Futurisms** — Grace Dillon's framework; structural parallel to Sinofuturism and Afrofuturism
- **Neo-Chinese Style (新中式)** — interior design / fashion register; commercial sibling to Sinofuturism
- **Silkpunk** — Ken Liu's term for Asian-rooted speculative aesthetics; literary-SF adjacent
- **Y3K** — the wider speculative-millennium aesthetic Sinofuturism is one regional flavour of

## Caveats

- ESL editing throughout; some phrasings are awkward but the conceptual through-line is clear.
- Citation apparatus is light: three external references (an Instagram post, the English Wikipedia "Sinofuturism" article, a Chinese-language essay on cssn.cn), plus one academic citation to Li Guangyi's *科幻导论* (*Introduction to Science Fiction*, Chongqing University Press, 2023).
- The wiki is community-edited and will drift over time — the version summarized here is the page as of June 2026 (revision retrievable via the Fandom revision history).
- "Socialist Cyberpunk" appears in the alternative-names field but is not developed elsewhere on the page. The single citation for it is an Instagram post.
