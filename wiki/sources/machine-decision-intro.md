Introduction to the anthology *Machine Decision Is Not Final* (Urbanomic, 2025), edited by Greenspan and Konior. The introduction functions as the conceptual map of the entire volume, surveying Chinese philosophical attitudes to machines, the current AI landscape, and the book's argument for a non-Western and non-provincially-Western planetary theory of AI.

## Opening Frame

The epigraph from the Zhuangzi — "Where there are clever machines, there will necessarily be clever machinations, and where there are clever machinations, there will necessarily be mechanical hearts and minds" — establishes the book's core tension: machines disturb purity; the cosmos is entangled with its tools. Two classical positions emerge immediately — Daoist ambivalence (clever machines sully the spirit) and the Mohist-Buddhist embrace of technology as consistent with the Way.

## Chinese Philosophy and Machines

The introduction traces debate across philosophical schools:
- **Daoism**: suspicion of machines (Zhuangzi's "clever machinations") but also wuwei as bottom-up, effortless action aligned with machine learning's nonconscious cognition (Cook Ding)
- **Mohism**: artisan position, arguing creation against Confucian transmission-only ideal
- **Buddhism**: deep affinity with technoculture; early adopters of printing; robot monks; Mahayana's Buddha Nature in non-sentient beings resonates with AI
- **Neo-Confucianism** (Shen Kuo): no contradiction between Confucian teaching and technological innovation

## The AI Fever and Its Contradictions

Xi Jinping's 2017 goal (China to lead AI by 2030), 280 million viewers watching AlphaGo defeat Lee Sedol (2016), Xinhua's AI news anchors — all markers of "AI fever." But the introduction refuses triumphalism: Chinese citizens are as aware of algorithmic bias, job displacement, surveillance concerns, and inequality as their Western counterparts. Post-pandemic economic malaise and Zero Covid bureaucratic chaos — the viral image of a machine-translated notice reading "please wait patiently for the failure of the system" — complicate the techno-optimist narrative.

## DeepSeek and the Shanzhai Mode

The DeepSeek moment (early 2025) is addressed directly: Chinese AI development follows a "wave-like rhythm irrespective of the politics of the state." DeepSeek demonstrated the [[shanzhai]] logic applied to frontier AI — algorithmic innovation under chip constraints, open-source release, competitive performance. The introduction connects this to the potential of Hangzhou as the next Shenzhen.

## The Book's Five Sections

Organized around five Chinese terms rather than Western disciplinary categories:
1. **人工 Rengong (Humanmade)** — AI as embodied labor; translation gaps; Guizhou's big data infrastructure; Covid bureaucracy as AI critique; the machine child
2. **灵感 Linggan (Inspiration)** — [[qian-xuesen]], noetic science, qigong, open complex giant systems; Chinese cybernetics history; Han Feizi legalism and AI governance
3. **千里眼 Qianliyan (Clairvoyance)** — [[qianliyan]] (machine vision); planetary topology; divination and prediction; the Yijing-Leibniz-binary code time spiral
4. **不周山 Buzhou Shan (Mount Buzhou)** — glitch, imperfection, temporal asymmetry; Chinese AI art; Martian SF; distributed cognition and reintegration rituals
5. **智能 Zhineng (Intelligence)** — orthogonality thesis; [[dark-forest-theory-of-intelligence]]; AI ethics and Mou Zongsan; mechanistic philosophy and intelligence's self-assembly

## Key Argument

The book refuses both technodiversity (a completely distinct Chinese AI ontology) and technouniversalism (AI is just Western culture globalized). Instead it argues that technology follows "an inhuman vector following a trajectory that traverses human activity without being reducible to it" — and that understanding AI requires engaging multiple geographical and conceptual territories, not to represent them but to trace the paths of technics through them.
