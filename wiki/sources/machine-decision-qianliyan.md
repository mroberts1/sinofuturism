Third section of *Machine Decision Is Not Final* (Urbanomic, 2025). Organized around 千里眼 (qianliyan, clairvoyance / thousand-mile eyes). Essays examine machine vision as distributed care infrastructure, AI neural nets and biological vision, planetary-scale surveillance topology, and the relationship between prediction and divination. See [[qianliyan]] for the core concept article.

## Gabriele de Seta — "Technologies of Clairvoyance: Chinese Lineages and Mythologies of Machine Vision"

De Seta opens with the qianliyan brand of video surveillance products — then traces the concept back to Daoist myth and forward through a history of optical technologies. The key intervention: qianliyan is **not primarily a state surveillance apparatus**. It is a distributed optical ecology in which civil servants, businesses, homeowners, parents, and children all participate in caring and watching — monitoring village roads, kitchen stoves, elderly parents, sleeping babies.

This distributed structure (borrowing "more-than-human sensory abilities" from the mythology) complicates the standard Western narrative of Chinese surveillance as monolithic state control. It also raises questions about who is watching whom and for what ends — care and control are entangled in the infrastructure.

## Blaise Agüera y Arcas — "The Mother of Ten Thousand Things"

A Google VP and AI researcher brings technical perspective. Agüera y Arcas shows that human visual cortex and artificial neural nets decompose vision in functionally equivalent ways: both separate **content from style**, both process visual information through hierarchical feature extraction. This suggests that machine vision is not a foreign mode of perception but a different instantiation of a biological pattern.

The title references the Daoist ten thousand things (万物, wanwu) — all things sharing in the Dao. The essay also addresses how AI-generated images challenge conventional judgements about realism and abstraction.

## Lukáš Likavčan — "The Governance of Habitability" (Interview)

As "smart" sensing infrastructures envelop the planet, the network model (nodes in a graph) becomes inadequate. Likavčan proposes **planetary topology** as an alternative: vertical, non-local, acknowledging spillovers across territorial boundaries.

Questions: Is a Chinese meteorological balloon an extension of China? Is Huawei? Is the Belt and Road a node in Chinese non-local space, or is Europe now a node in Chinese space? These cannot be answered within a network model. A planetary topology allows perception of "non-fixed localities" — seeing how computation and infrastructure exceed the borders drawn around them.

## Steve Goodman, Anna Greenspan, Lawrence Lek — "Sinofuturism and AI: A Conversation"

A wide-ranging discussion that touches on the Yijing–Leibniz–binary code connection (the "retrochronic Sino-European time spiral"), the permeability of sinofuturist prediction and divination, and the aesthetics of machine intelligence.

Key exchange on closed environments and walled gardens at all scales — from user authentication to geopolitical internet governance: "Which systems can communicate with each other and which ones can't?" The Great Firewall as technical-cultural boundary that has paradoxically enabled the development of a distinctive and competitive Chinese media ecosystem.

On prediction vs. prophecy: "In ancient times they would try to predict crop harvests or whether it will be a cold winter or a bountiful spring, whereas now it's about what might happen in the next nanosecond of trading. The resolution has increased, and the time division has shrunk, but the impossibility of certainty is as present as ever." At the outer edges of complexity, forecasting and fortune-telling converge — and it is this "unexpected, incalculable element of surprise" that sinofuturist co-creation with machine intelligence aims to cultivate.
