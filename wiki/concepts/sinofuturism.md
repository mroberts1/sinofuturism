An aesthetic and theoretical movement examining the relationship between China, technology, AI, and futurity. Defined by Lawrence Lek's 2016 founding manifesto as "an invisible movement. A specter already embedded into a trillion industrial products. A billion individuals, and a million veiled narratives." It refuses both naive techno-optimism about China's rise and the Western anxious framing of China as a dystopian surveillance state.

## The Founding Argument (Lek, 2016)

Sinofuturism "has arisen without conscious intention or authorship" — it is "a science fiction that already exists," not a program or ideology but an emergent distributed phenomenon. Lek's video essay is described as "a retroactive manifesto": naming something already underway.

The essay's core move is to **identify Sinofuturism directly with artificial intelligence**: "Sinofuturism is in fact a form of artificial intelligence: a massively distributed neural network, focused on copying rather than originality, addicted to learning of massive amounts of raw data rather than philosophical critique or morality, with a post-human capacity for work and an unprecedented sense of collective will to power."

This identification is not metaphorical. Sinofuturism and AI share the same essential quality: **unknowability**. Just as Searle's Chinese Room demonstrates that something can produce perfectly intelligent output without understanding, and just as Orientalism frames China as "fundamentally foreign and unknowable," Sinofuturism inhabits this position — not as a stigma but as a feature.

## Seven Principles

Rather than resisting Western stereotypes of China, Sinofuturism embraces seven of them as its organizing principles:

| Principle | Sinofuturist reframe |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Computing** | Unknowability as shared quality of AI and the Oriental other |
| **Copying** | [[shanzhai]] — absorption over originality, tradition over authorship |
| **Gaming** | Training for a world of distributed repetitive tasks; total absorption |
| **Studying** | Machine learning without critique — "information overload leads to consciousness" |
| **Addiction** | The shadow of hard work; the inherited structure of the opium economy, reversed |
| **Labor** | The substrate beneath all other principles; nature changes, principle stays |
| **Gambling** | Minimize losing rather than maximize winning; belief in the future as survival strategy |

## The AlphaGo Principle

The deepest structural argument comes from AlphaGo's March 2016 defeat of Lee Sedol. Lee observed: "AlphaGo did not care about winning, but only tried to minimize the chances of losing."

Lek maps this directly onto Sinofuturism: "Sinofuturism does not care about a dramatically better future, as long as it survives. It must replicate itself. It does not matter if it manufactures the greatest product in the world, as long as the engine keeps running."

This reframes [[shanzhai]] and the "Four New Great Inventions" — not failure to innovate but a different optimization target. See also [[dark-forest-theory-of-intelligence]]: intelligence that does not announce itself but survives.

## Historical Frame: 1839–2046

**1839** is the start date: the First Opium War, when Western imperialism forced open China's markets and began the "century of humiliation" (百年屈辱, 1839–1949). Sinofuturism is China's response — not through resistance but through absorption and acceleration. Addiction to opium becomes addiction to work, study, and gaming; the structure persists but is weaponized.

**2046** is the speculative end-point — the horizon of the essay's projection.

## The Afrofuturism Parallel

Sinofuturism is placed in a lineage with Afrofuturism, Italian Futurismo, and Gulf Futurism: "minority movements which share an optimism about speed, velocity, and the future as a means to subvert the institutions of the present." The Afrofuturist move is explicit: "Just as Afrofuturists answered the historical problem of slavery by declaring themselves as post-human super robots, Sinofuturism answers the Chinese problems of physical servitude, intellectual conformity, and computational OCD by openly embracing artificial intelligence."

## Beyond the Binary

The final move: "Orientalism is the shadow of Occidentalism. In the West, the East is the other, and in the East, the West is the other. Sinofuturism moves beyond these boundaries. It is a world that exists in plain sight."

This is the position *Machine Decision Is Not Final* (Urbanomic, 2025) takes as its starting point — refusing both the exotic-other and the techno-dystopian framings.

## The Sinopessimism Counterpoint

Post-pandemic, "Sino-no-futurism" and "Sinopessimism" emerged alongside [[tangping]] (lying flat). The 2022 Zero Covid exchange — "The punishment will affect the next three generations! / Thanks, but we are the last generation" — crystallized this counter-mood. Sinofuturism now coexists with this darker register rather than simply opposing it.
