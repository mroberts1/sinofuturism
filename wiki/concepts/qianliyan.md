千里眼 — "thousand-mile eyes." A Daoist mythological concept describing supernatural far-seeing, adopted as the brand name for a Chinese video surveillance product line, and used by Gabriele de Seta to frame a broader analysis of machine vision in China that moves beyond simple state-centric surveillance narratives.

## Mythological Origin

Qianliyan is derived from a Daoist myth: a deified Shang dynasty General who serves as guardian to Mazu, the goddess of the sea. In Buddhist representations, the divine faculties of seeing and hearing — "more-than-human sensory abilities" — attach to qianliyan as a figure of expanded perception that transcends individual human limits.

## Machine Vision Beyond the State

De Seta's analysis refuses the dominant Western framing of Chinese surveillance as a top-down state apparatus. Instead, qianliyan describes a **distributed optical governance** in which:
- Civil servants monitor village infrastructure
- Businesses watch storefronts and employees
- Homeowners surveil their properties
- Parents check on children
- Children check on elderly parents

Surveillance is not only repressive control but a networked care structure — distributed, bottom-up, entangled with domestic life.

## The History of Optics as Intelligence

De Seta traces a genealogy: towers and telescopes → sensing devices at the optics/cybernetics intersection → AI-enabled vision. Blaise Agüera y Arcas adds that AI neural networks and human visual cortex decompose vision in functionally equivalent ways — both separate content from style — suggesting machine vision is not an alien mode of perception but a different instantiation of biological pattern.

## Planetary Scale

Lukáš Likavčan argues the "network" model (nodes in a graph) is no longer adequate to describe AI-enhanced optical governance. A more useful model is **planetary topology**: non-local, vertical, acknowledging spillovers across territorial boundaries. Is a Chinese meteorological balloon an extension of China? Is Huawei? Is the Belt and Road Initiative? These are qianliyan questions at planetary scale.

## Prediction and Prophecy

In "Sinofuturism and AI," Goodman, Greenspan, and Lek discuss how AI farsight at the edge of complexity is indistinguishable from divination: "In ancient times they would try to predict crop harvests... whereas now it's about what might happen in the next nanosecond of trading. The resolution has increased... but the impossibility of certainty is as present as ever." The Yijing (I Ching), introduced to Leibniz by Jesuits, sits at the origin of binary code — a retrochronic Sino-European time spiral connecting divination and computation.
