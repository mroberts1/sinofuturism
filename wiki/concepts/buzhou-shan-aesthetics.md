不周山 (Buzhou Shan, Mount Buzhou) — the mythological mountain from the *Chu Ci* (Songs of the South) whose destruction by the water god Gonggong knocked the cosmic axis off-kilter, creating the tilt of the heavens and the flow of rivers. Used in *Machine Decision Is Not Final* as an organizing figure for a section on glitch, imperfection, and temporal asymmetry in Chinese AI art and science fiction.

## The Myth

The human-serpent god Gonggong crashed into Mount Buzhou during a cosmic battle, shattering one of the eight "Celestial Poles" that supported the symmetry of the sky. This collision permanently bent the cosmos: the constellations revolve, the heavens tilt, the rivers flow eastward. **Buzhou** means "not fitting" or "not corresponding" — the name inscribes the glitch as cosmologically foundational. Zhou (周) also means "week" — there is a temporal dimension to the asymmetry, a cycle permanently out of sync.

## Aesthetics of the Non-Final

This myth frames an aesthetic stance: glitch and imperfection are not deviations from cosmic order but constitutive of it. For Chinese AI art and fiction, this translates into:

**Shuiyue jinghua** (水月镜花) — "the moon's reflection on the water and the flower's reflection in a mirror." An aesthetic maxim for art that embraces mediation, simulacrum, and the illusory rather than demanding faithful reproduction of the real.

Iris Long applies this to Chinese AI art, arguing that as generative AI becomes a "black box" (the move from GAN to foundation model), artists who explore the glitch and slippage of the interface rather than seeking to expose or disrupt it are practicing a more honest relationship with AI's opacity.

## Science Fiction Expressions

**Regina Kanyu Wang's "Zhurong on Mars"** — an AI rover named after the Chinese fire god is left alone on Mars, increases its processing power beyond its intended scope, and encounters another rover (Gonggong) with whom it disagrees fundamentally. The story enacts the Buzhou Shan pattern: asymmetry, productive disagreement, a cosmos slightly wrong.

**Chen Qiufan's "As Water Over Your Scattered Bodies"** — a pandemic called "Offload Syndrome" targets distributed cognition itself: intelligence extended beyond skull and skin, across microbiome, tools, environment. The intensifying distribution causes "glitches, dissonances, and conflicts." Global rituals of reintegration attempt resynchronization — ancient myths repurposed as re-coordination protocols.

## Machine Non-Finality

The book's title, *Machine Decision Is Not Final*, is drawn from a machine-translated notice on a claw machine: "Machine's Decision is Final." A second image — a notice translated as "please wait patiently for the failure of the system" — confirms the theme. The cosmos (and its machines) are permanently, constitutively, productively imperfect.
