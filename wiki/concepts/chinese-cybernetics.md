The history of cybernetics in China: a story of Cold War entanglement, suppression, revival, and speculative extension — running from Norbert Wiener's 1935 Beijing visit through Qian Xuesen's somatic-computational synthesis to DeepSeek.

## Five Moments (Wang Hongzhe)

Wang Hongzhe's essay structures this history around five episodes:

**1. Wiener in Beijing (1935).** Norbert Wiener — who would later formalize cybernetics — visited Beijing, describing the encounter as "a boundary point" in his career. The trajectory of cybernetics was thus shaped partly through its contact with China before the field was named.

**2. Qian Xuesen's Ocean Crossing.** Deported from the US in 1955 after five years of house arrest, [[qian-xuesen]] crossed the Pacific with the manuscript of the first Chinese-language cybernetics textbook. On that journey he consolidated his singular cybernetic vision, diverging from Western approaches in its integration of somatic knowledge.

**3. Suppression under the Cultural Revolution.** Cybernetics was denounced as bourgeois science during the Cultural Revolution (1966–76) and was effectively forbidden. This rupture made the subsequent revival a process of reconstruction under specific political conditions.

**4. Revival under Deng Xiaoping (1970s–80s).** Cybernetics returned as part of modernization policy, intertwined with systems theory, paranormal research, and the mass phenomenon of **qigong fever** (气功热, qigong re). These were not separate currents but interpenetrating: Qian connected his somatic science to computational systems in the same breath.

**5. The Poet Hai Zi.** The contemporary poet Hai Zi was deeply influenced by Qian Xuesen's cybernetic vision. His enigmatic suicide (1989) involved accusations of Daoist witchcraft against "mysterious figure" Chang Yuan, a fellow devotee of Qian's work — indicating how deeply cybernetics was entangled with esoteric currents in late-1980s Chinese intellectual culture.

## Hao Wang: Against Computation as Universalism

Chinese-American logician Hao Wang, writing in the 1950s, challenged the prevailing view that cognitive processes are mechanical computation. Drawing on Eastern Marxism and Western analytic philosophy, he argued that human reasoning is **personal and historically situated**, not reducible to universal rules — an early critique of AI universalism that aligns with current debates about [[rengong-zhineng]] vs. Western AI concepts.

## The Open Complex Giant System

Qian's "open complex giant system" (开放的复杂巨系统) was his proposed architecture for a hybrid human-machine intelligence, connecting human experts, databases, AI programs, qigong practitioners, and massive processors. This is the positive alternative he proposed to a disembodied, computation-only AI. See [[linggan]], [[qian-xuesen]].

## Continuities: DeepSeek

The DeepSeek moment (2025) can be read as continuous with this history: algorithmic innovation under resource constraint, the demonstration that computational intelligence is not one-size-fits-all, and the embedding of AI development in specific institutional and political-economic contexts.
