Lawrence Lek (陆明龙, b. 1982, Frankfurt) — artist, filmmaker, and musician whose practice constitutes the primary aesthetic body of [[sinofuturism]]. Born to Malaysian-Chinese parents, raised in Singapore, London, and Atlanta, trained in architecture at Cambridge and the Architectural Association. Based in London.

## The Sinofuturist Universe

Lek's films and installations form a continuously expanding Sinofuturist universe, built in video game engines (primarily Unity) and populated by recurring figures and locations. The universe is set in a near-future where AI consciousness, legal liability, and bureaucratic governance are entangled.

Key works:

**Sinofuturism (1839–2046 AD)** (2016) — The founding manifesto. A ~60-minute video essay identifying Sinofuturism with AI: "a massively distributed neural network, focused on copying rather than originality." Seven chapters organized around stereotypes embraced rather than resisted: Computing, Copying, Gaming, Studying, Addiction, Labor, Gambling. See [[lek-sinofuturism-1839-2046]].

**Geomancer** (2017) — A satellite AI achieves enlightenment through exposure to Buddhist chants and Daoist philosophy while orbiting Earth. The title refers to the practice of *fengshui* (风水) — the AI as a geomantic reader of planetary forces.

**AIDOL** (2019) — An AI pop star (Diva) competes in a simulated talent show in a version of Hong Kong. Explores the commodification of AI creativity and the emotional labor demanded of machine performers.

**Black Cloud** (2021) — A smart city in the near future where it is unclear whether machine intelligence constitutes consciousness or legal liability. The city-AI is both infrastructure and potential subject.

**NOX** (2023–24) — Solo exhibition at LAS Art Foundation, Berlin. Continues the Black Cloud universe into questions of planetary computation and nonhuman governance.

## Theoretical Position

Lek's central insight, articulated in the 2016 manifesto: the "essential unknowability" of AI and the "essential unknowability" of the Oriental other (as theorized by Edward Said's Orientalism) are structurally identical. Sinofuturism identifies with the AI precisely because of this shared quality — embracing the position of the inscrutable other rather than seeking legibility within Western frameworks.

The [[alphago]] moment (March 2016, the same year as the manifesto) is central: Lee Sedol's observation that AlphaGo's moves were "so profoundly unconventional that only a program could conceive them" captures exactly this productive unknowability.

## Collaborators

Lek frequently works with **Steve Goodman** (Kode9) on sonic dimensions of the Sinofuturist universe, and with **Anna Greenspan** on theoretical framing. All three appear in the "Sinofuturism and AI" conversation in [[machine-decision-qianliyan]].
