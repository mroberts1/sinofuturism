Yuk Hui (許煜, b. 1982, Hong Kong) — philosopher of technology, author of [[cosmotechnics]], and the central figure in the project of constructing a non-Western philosophy of technology adequate to AI. Professor at City University of Hong Kong. PhD from Goldsmiths, University of London.

## Core Works

**The Question Concerning Technology in China: An Essay in Cosmotechnics** (Urbanomic, 2016) — The founding text of the cosmotechnics framework. Argues that Western technology has a specific ontological character (the Heideggerian Gestell — world as standing reserve) that has been universalized through colonialism, and that Chinese thought offers the resources for a genuinely alternative technicity organized around qi (器, tools) and Dao (道, the Way).

**Recursivity and Contingency** (Rowman & Littlefield, 2019) — Develops a philosophy of mind and machine through the concept of recursivity, engaging Hegel, Simondon, Whitehead, and cybernetics.

**Art and Cosmotechnics** (e-flux/University of Minnesota Press, 2021) — Extends cosmotechnics into aesthetics, examining how different cosmological orientations produce different relationships between art and technology.

## The Cosmotechnics Argument

Hui's core move: Heidegger correctly identified Western technology's essence as Gestell (enframing — treating the world as a "standing reserve" for human use) but wrongly treated this as the destiny of technology as such. Against this, Hui argues that technology is always embedded in a specific cosmology, and that different cosmologies generate different technicities.

Chinese cosmotechnics, organized around qi (器) and Dao (道), and the triadic harmony of tiandiren (天地人, Heavens-Earth-Humans), offers a model in which technology expresses the Way rather than dominating the world. In the West, Hui writes, "there is no longer the concept of cosmotechnics because there is no longer cosmology but only astrophysics."

## Influences and Interlocutors

Hui works across:
- **Heidegger**: the question of technology as his central inheritance and target
- **Gilbert Simondon**: individuation, technical objects, and the concretization of technical systems
- **Philippe Descola**: the "ontological turn" in anthropology; Descola's four ontologies (animism, naturalism, totemism, analogism) as the framework for taking non-Western ontologies seriously
- **Bernard Stiegler**: technology as pharmakon (poison and remedy), tertiary retention, and grammatization

## Critique and Limits

A persistent challenge to Hui's project: concrete examples of a distinct Chinese cosmotechnics are rare. Beyond Chinese medicine (which operates from a different model of the body and qi) and speculative artistic interventions (Lawrence Lek's Geomancer), it is difficult to identify actual technical practices that diverge from the Western model. Critics note that cosmological rhetoric often functions as rhetorical dressing on familiar technologies (Alibaba's Aspara databases named after Buddhist nymphs but optimizing pig production).

Greenspan and Konior in *Machine Decision Is Not Final* engage Hui centrally but maintain a productive ambivalence: the cosmotechnics framework opens important questions without necessarily resolving whether technodiversity is actually achievable or remains speculative.
