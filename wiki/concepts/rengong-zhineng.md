人工智能 — literally "humanmade knowing ability." The Chinese term for artificial intelligence, whose connotations diverge substantially from the English "artificial intelligence."

## The Translation Gap

- **人工** (rengong): composed of 人 (person) and 工 (work/labor) — emphasizes embodied human labor and material craft, not fakery or artifice
- **智能** (zhineng): knowing ability, intelligence as practical capacity
- **Artificial** in English derives from "artifice" — implies inauthenticity, deception, the counterfeit

When Elon Musk and Jack Ma discussed AI in 2019, Shuang L. Frost observes, the term evoked "entirely different constellations of meanings." For Ma, rengong alluded to human manual labor and a Marxist-inflected cybernetics. For Musk, "artificiality" conjured something inhuman and threatening.

## Rengong as Human Computer

Liu Cixin's *Three-Body Problem* captures the imaginary of rengong vividly: Emperor Qin Shi Huang's army of thirty million laborers is organized into a gigantic human computer, each soldier signaling input/output with black and white flags. Intelligence here is the coordination of bodies — distributed, collaborative, material — not the simulation of an individual mind.

This understanding of intelligence as **coordinated organisation and collaborative production** (Bratton) contrasts with the Western individualist framing, where AI must pass as a single human consciousness to be recognized as real.

## Implications

Benjamin Bratton argues this difference has deep implications: Chinese theory of AI locates intelligence in the coordination of minds and bodies into "common compositions and acts of worldmaking." AI is not primarily asked to mirror an individual human mind, but to relate to the forces that shape collective production and governance.

This makes the [[chinese-cybernetics]] history — Qian Xuesen's "open complex giant systems" combining human experts, databases, and AI — directly continuous with the rengong-zhineng imaginary.
