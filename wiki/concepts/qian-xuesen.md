钱学森 (1911–2009). Engineer, cyberneticist, and state hero — the pivotal figure in the entangled histories of Chinese and American computation. Celebrated in China as the "father of Chinese rockets" and the "father of Chinese cybernetics," his biography encapsulates the violent geopolitical disentanglement of US-China techno-scientific collaboration.

## American Formation

Qian trained in California, co-founded Caltech's **Jet Propulsion Laboratory**, and was among the leading aerospace engineers in the US. During the Second Red Scare (McCarthyism), he was deemed a security threat and placed under **house arrest in California for five years**. He used this detention to write the first Chinese-language textbook on cybernetics.

## Return to China

Deported to China in 1955, Qian arrived just before the Sino-Soviet split and the Cultural Revolution — a period when cybernetics would fall into disrepute. He played a central role in China's missile, computer, satellite, and space programmes, and is the person most responsible for bringing systems engineering and cybernetics to the PRC.

## Noetic Science and Linggan

Qian's most distinctive intellectual contribution was **思维科学** (siwei kexue, "noetic science"): a proposed discipline for studying inspiration, intuition, and creativity — the aspects of human thought that resist algorithmic reduction. See [[linggan]].

His concept of **开放的复杂巨系统** (kaifang de fuza juxitong, "open complex giant system") envisioned a hybrid human-machine intelligence combining:
- Human domain experts
- Databases
- AI programs
- Processors capable of billions of computations per second

This was not disembodied AI but a somatic-computational synthesis, connected to the qigong and body-science (人体科学, renti kexue) movements of the 1980s.

## Cultural Resonances

Wang Hongzhe traces Qian's influence on poet **Hai Zi**, a significant figure in modern Chinese technoculture, who was deeply engaged with Qian's cybernetic vision. Hai Zi's enigmatic suicide in 1989 was associated with accusations of Daoist witchcraft — indicating how deeply Chinese cybernetics was intertwined with esoteric and paranormal currents of the era.
